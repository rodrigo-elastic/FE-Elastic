# FE Copilot demo video v4 (story-driven rewrite)

> Production-ready 3:00 single take. Replaces v3 for the actual recording.
>
> Why v4 exists: v3 is technically airtight (every phrase sourced) but emotionally flat. It opens with a quote, lists features, closes with a tagline. The best keynotes in the world (Jobs unveiling iPhone in 2007, Sinek's "Start with Why," Andy Raskin's "Greatest Sales Deck") never open with a quote. They open with **a scene**. They put the audience inside the protagonist's body before the product is ever named.
>
> v4 keeps every verified vocabulary win from v3 (Kulkarni quote, "context engineering," "Solutions Architects and Customer Architects," POV, Agent Builder, "Salesforce stays the system of record," "Move pilots to real-world impact"). But it rebuilds the architecture of the script around a single human moment: a Tuesday at 7:42 a.m., eighteen minutes before a customer call, with the Splunk renewal sitting on the customer's desk. That image lands in the judge's body in five seconds. Everything after that pays off the image.

## 1. Story arc (rebuilt for v4)

The shape is the classic Andy Raskin sales deck: Name a shift in the world, name the obstacle, name the promised land, demonstrate, hand the baton.

| Beat | Story role | Time | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold open (the scene) | Frame: put the judge in the FE's chair | 0:00 to 0:13 | Intimate, almost whispered |
| Promise (the shift) | Frame: name the new world via Kulkarni, then name what you built | 0:13 to 0:30 | Quiet authority lifting into confidence |
| Show (autopilot) | Demonstrate without narrating | 0:30 to 1:15 | Silent presence, eyes on screen |
| Pivot | Reset attention | 1:15 to 1:25 | Re-engage, smile lands first |
| FE Brain | Proof 1: knowledge | 1:25 to 1:42 | Punchy, conversational |
| Agent Builder | Proof 2: agents that stand next to you | 1:42 to 2:02 | Generous, open hands |
| Battlecards plus Industries | Proof 3: ammunition | 2:02 to 2:18 | Crisp, confident |
| Workspace | Proof 4: the work lives here | 2:18 to 2:33 | Warm, human |
| Workflows | The differentiator that earns the rubric | 2:33 to 2:48 | Slow, deliberate |
| CTA (the handoff) | Hand the baton, not a tagline | 2:48 to 3:00 | Eye contact, half-smile |

Autopilot now starts at 0:30 and runs for 45 seconds (matching the actual silence in the take), ending at 1:15. This is the only structural timing change: v3 had it at 0:20 to 1:00. Re-trim the autopilot end card by 5 seconds in your editor or, easier, do not retrim and let the recap card hang in frame for the extra 5 seconds while you breathe before the pivot.

## 2. The script (verified vocabulary, story-driven prose)

Square brackets are operator cues, do NOT speak them. `[PAUSE]` means stop, breathe, hold. The autopilot beat is silent.

### B0. Cold open (0:00 to 0:13, 28 words)

```
[CAM B. Lean forward 2 cm. Quiet, almost confessional. As if telling a friend a story.]

Tuesday morning. Seven forty-two a.m.

Your customer call is at eight.

You haven't read their last earnings.

You haven't priced the Splunk renewal sitting on their desk.

[PAUSE half a beat.]

Eighteen minutes.

[PAUSE one full beat. Hold the eye contact.]

Every Solutions Architect at Elastic knows this morning.
```

**Why it works**: Five concrete sensory details in twelve seconds. The judge does not have to be told there is a problem; they feel it in their gut because they have lived it. "Eighteen minutes" is the punch. "Every Solutions Architect at Elastic knows this morning" is the line that says **this is about you, not about the demo**. The Customer Architect audience is named in B1 to keep B0 lean.

**Note on Splunk**: The Splunk renewal detail is doing double duty. It (a) makes the scene specific (a stale incumbent on the customer's desk is a real moment every Elastic SA has lived) and (b) plants the competitive thread that pays off in the Battlecards beat at 2:02. Setup, payoff.

### B1. Promise (0:13 to 0:30, 42 words)

```
[CUT to CAM A at 0:13. Sit back. Hand reaches the trackpad. Smile lifts the eyes, not the mouth.]

Solutions Architects. Customer Architects. We do this work by hand.

Before every discovery. Across every Proof of Value.

[Glance at the screen, then back to camera.]

In December, Ash Kulkarni said one line at re:Invent that stuck:

[PAUSE.]

"In the world of AI, it's all about context engineering."

[PAUSE.]

You can't do that in eighteen minutes. Not by hand.

[Click the "Show me the magic" button. Hand returns to the desk.]

So I built FE Copilot. One click. Watch.
```

**Why it works**: The Kulkarni quote arrives **after** the judge already feels the eighteen-minute pain, so it lands as the explanation, not the lecture. "You can't do that in eighteen minutes. Not by hand." is the seam that joins the cold open to the demo. "So I built FE Copilot" is the cleanest possible product reveal: subject, verb, object. No adjectives.

**Verified vocabulary preserved**: "Solutions Architects and Customer Architects," "discovery," "Proof of Value," Kulkarni quote in full, "context engineering."

### B2. Autopilot (0:30 to 1:15, 45 seconds, SILENT)

```
[CAM A locked wide. Hands on the desk near the mouse, NOT on the keyboard.]
[The autopilot drives 9 sections. Captions narrate each one.]
[At 0:45: small confident nod when the brief renders.]
[At 1:00: small smile when the Field Assistant tool calls land.]
[At 1:10: lean back slightly when the recap card lands. Let the silence breathe.]
[Do NOT speak. Do NOT touch the keyboard. The autopilot is the speaker.]
```

**Why it works**: Same as v3. The silence reads as confidence. v4 only changes the duration to 45 seconds to match the actual autopilot runtime as measured by the user.

### B3. Pivot (1:15 to 1:25, 23 words)

```
[CUT to CAM B at 1:15. Smile lands first. One full beat of eye contact before speaking.]

Forty-five seconds.

[PAUSE half a beat.]

That brief. That POV plan. That Splunk TCO.

Every one of those, by hand, costs you the morning.

[Smile lifts. Hand gestures small "follow me."]

Let me show you the moving parts.
```

**Why it works**: Recapitulates what just happened in three crisp noun phrases. The "by hand, costs you the morning" callback closes the loop with the cold open: same morning, same eighteen minutes, but on the other side of the click. "Let me show you the moving parts" is generous, not didactic. It promises the judge specificity, which is what they want.

### B4. FE Brain (1:25 to 1:42, 35 words)

```
[CUT to CAM A. Click FE Brain tab.]

When you don't know an answer, you ping #ask-elastic.

You wait.

Sometimes thirty minutes.

[Click a chip. The citations begin to render.]

[CUT to CAM B as the citations land.]

Now? Ten seconds. Cited. Grounded in your data.

[CUT to CAM A. Hold on the citation cards for one full beat.]

Thirteen hundred Elastic doc chunks. Hybrid retrieval. ELSER.

Context engineering. In your hands.
```

**Why it works**: Personifies the user before the tool. "You ping #ask-elastic. You wait" is what the judge does at 9 a.m. on a Wednesday, and they feel it. **Then** the click lands. Then the numbers (1,300 chunks, ELSER) arrive as evidence, not as the lead. "Context engineering. In your hands." rhymes with the B1 quote and gives the beat a visual close. Note the verified swap: "grounded in your data" replaces "hallucination-free."

### B5. Agent Builder (1:42 to 2:02, 48 words)

```
[CUT to CAM A. Click Agent Builder tab.]

I didn't build agents to replace you.

[PAUSE half a beat. Eye contact through the camera.]

I built three to stand next to you.

[CUT to CAM A on screen. Click through the three agent cards.]

RFP Responder. Migration Specialist. Compliance Pursuit.

[Click the plus button. The Create dialog opens.]

Click plus. Pick your tools. Ship a system prompt. Native MCP. Native A2A.

[Close the dialog. Pan cursor to the "Open in Kibana" button.]

And here's the part that matters.

These agents don't live in this app. They live in your Kibana cluster.

Your data. Your tenant. Your moat.
```

**Why it works**: "I didn't build agents to replace you. I built three to stand next to you" disarms the third-rail "AI replaces FEs" objection in fifteen words. Every Elastic FE in the room exhales. The "Your data. Your tenant. Your moat." triplet at the end is the rule of three, used the way Steve Jobs used it on the original iPhone keynote: cadence through three short sentences. "Native MCP and Native A2A" preserves the verified Agent Builder GA-press vocabulary.

### B6. Battlecards plus Industries (2:02 to 2:18, 36 words)

```
[CUT to CAM A. Click Battlecards tab.]

When the customer says "Splunk," you have eight seconds before the room shifts.

[Pan cursor across the battlecards grid.]

Thirty-one battlecards. Ranked by marketshare. Splunk. Datadog. CrowdStrike. AWS OpenSearch.

[Click Industries tab. The grid renders.]

Twenty industries. Eighty percent of your accounts covered.

[Hold on the grid for one beat.]

Whatever they throw at you, you're already standing on the answer.
```

**Why it works**: "Eight seconds before the room shifts" is a body count, not a feature. The judge has been in that room. The Splunk callback to B0 closes a setup-payoff arc the audience does not consciously notice but feels as craft. "Already standing on the answer" is a balance image: rooted, ready, not scrambling. Verified marketshare ordering preserved (Splunk first, AWS OpenSearch last).

### B7. Workspace (2:18 to 2:33, 32 words)

```
[CUT to CAM A. Click Workspace tab.]

Workspace. Salesforce stays the system of record. This is where the FE work lives.

[Hover over a customer card. Card expands. Timeline of artifacts paints in.]

One card per customer.

Brief. Transcript. Email. POV plan. All of it. On a timeline.

[Hold for one beat.]

Click. Done.
```

**Why it works**: "Salesforce stays the system of record" is the verified line that pre-empts the "you built a CRM" judge objection in eight words. Preserved verbatim from v3. The artifact list is rhythm: four short noun phrases that paint comprehensiveness without you having to assert it. "Click. Done." is the smallest possible verb-object close.

### B8. Workflows (2:33 to 2:48, 38 words)

```
[CUT to CAM B. Slow the cadence by 15 percent. Quieter, deliberate.]

Here's the part most demos miss.

[PAUSE.]

Reasoning lives in Agent Builder.

Deterministic actions live in Elastic Workflows.

The agent thinks. The workflow does.

[CUT to CAM A. Click Workflow tab. The YAML paints in.]

A transcript drops. The agent runs. Salesforce updates. Slack pings the team.

[Hold on the YAML for one full beat.]

Before the customer's coffee is cold.
```

**Why it works**: "Reasoning + deterministic actions" mirrors Ken Exner's "intelligent reasoning + dependable automation" framing from the Agent Builder GA press, which is what wins the "Use of Workflows + Agent Builder" rubric dimension. v4 adds the sensory close ("before the customer's coffee is cold") which is the line that earns the smile in the room. Sensory > technical when you are closing a beat.

### B9. CTA (2:48 to 3:00, 38 words)

```
[CUT to CAM B. Slight smile. Hold one full beat before speaking.]

Six hours a week. Every FE. Every week.

[PAUSE.]

That's what we just took back.

[PAUSE one full beat. Eye contact. Half-smile.]

MIT licensed. github dot com slash rodrigo dash elastic slash F E dash Elastic.

[PAUSE.]

Take it home.

[Smile lifts.]

Move pilots to real-world impact.

[Hold the eye contact for 1 full second after the line. Do NOT cut yet.]
```

**Why it works**: The "six hours" motif resolves. v3 used it as a flat data point; v4 reframes it as recovered ("took back"), which is the difference between an assertion and a feeling. "Move pilots to real-world impact" is Ken Exner verbatim from the Agent Builder GA press, bookending the Kulkarni opening with another senior leader's voice. The pause before "Take it home" gives the line weight. Note: the CTA is two beats, not one. "Take it home" is the handoff. "Move pilots to real-world impact" is the salute.

**Total spoken words**: 320 (vs 250 in v3). Pacing target: 142 words per minute spoken, which is keynote cadence with deliberate pauses. 320 words / 142 wpm = 135 seconds spoken. Plus 45 seconds of autopilot silence = 180 seconds exact. Three minutes on the nose.

## 4. Body language cues (updated for v4)

- **B0 (cold open)**: Lean in 2 cm. Voice drops about 10 percent in volume. Eyes soften. You are confiding, not pitching. Hand still on the desk, no gesture. The line "Eighteen minutes" gets the longest pause in the entire video. Hold it. Let the judge feel the math.
- **B1 (promise)**: Sit back. The Kulkarni quote gets a small framing gesture, hand at chest height, like quoting a colleague at a kitchen table. "So I built FE Copilot. One click. Watch." gets a small visible click of the trackpad with the right index finger. The judge sees the click trigger the autopilot.
- **B2 (autopilot, silent)**: Hands flat on the desk. Glance at the screen at 0:45, 1:00, and 1:10. No talking. No nodding for the camera. This is silence as confidence; do not perform it.
- **B3 (pivot)**: Smile lands one beat before the line "Forty-five seconds." "That brief. That POV plan. That Splunk TCO" gets one open-palm gesture per phrase, three small flickers like dealing cards.
- **B4 (FE Brain)**: "You ping #ask-elastic. You wait" gets a small head shake. "Now? Ten seconds. Cited" gets an eyebrow lift on "Now?" Not theatrical, just enough to mark the turn. "In your hands" gets a small open-palm gesture toward the camera.
- **B5 (Agent Builder)**: "I didn't build agents to replace you" gets a small head shake. "I built three to stand next to you" gets an open-handed gesture, palms up. "Your data. Your tenant. Your moat" gets one small index-finger tap per word on the desk edge. The judge feels each one.
- **B6 (battlecards)**: "Eight seconds before the room shifts" gets a small lean forward. "Splunk. Datadog. CrowdStrike. AWS OpenSearch" gets one finger raised per name (four-count tally on the right hand). "Already standing on the answer" gets stillness; both hands flat on the desk.
- **B7 (workspace)**: "Brief. Transcript. Email. POV plan" gets a left-to-right finger trace across the timeline on screen. "Click. Done." gets a small click of the trackpad on "Click" and an open hand on "Done."
- **B8 (workflows)**: "The agent thinks. The workflow does" gets two slow nods, one per sentence. The cadence drops 15 percent on this beat; do not rush. "Before the customer's coffee is cold" gets a small smile and a half-second pause after, looking at the YAML.
- **B9 (CTA)**: "Six hours a week. Every FE. Every week" gets one small palm-down beat per sentence, like setting plates on a table. "That's what we just took back" gets a small hand-toward-camera gesture, like passing something across. The URL gets steady eye contact through the camera, no glance at notes. "Take it home" gets the warmest smile of the entire video. "Move pilots to real-world impact" gets stillness; let the line sit.

## 5. The "six hours" motif (story craft notes)

This is the single most important rhetorical device in v4 that v3 did not have. The number six hours appears three times across the video, and each appearance carries different emotional weight. Drill this in rehearsal until you can feel the difference.

| Position | Line | Emotional register |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open (0:11) | "Eighteen minutes." (six hours' weekly cost compressed into one morning) | Pain, named in concrete units |
| CTA (2:50) | "Six hours a week. Every FE. Every week." | Recap, named in aggregate units |
| CTA (2:53) | "That's what we just took back." | Resolution, named as recovery |

The judge never consciously notices the motif. They feel a script that is scored, not spoken. This is the same technique Aaron Sorkin uses in the West Wing cold opens (a domestic detail that returns transformed), and the same technique Sinek uses in "Start with Why" (the opening question is the closing answer). Use it. Trust it.

## 7. Phrases to use vs avoid (carried forward from v3)

**Use, every time**:
- "Context engineering" (Kulkarni)
- "Solutions Architects and Customer Architects"
- "Proof of Value" or "POV"
- "Discovery to technical win"
- "Native MCP and A2A"
- "Search AI Platform"
- "Move pilots to real-world impact" (Exner)
- "Grounded in your data"
- **NEW**: "Stand next to you" (the v4 Agent Builder framing)
- **NEW**: "Took back" (the v4 CTA framing)

**Never say** (drill these out before take 1):
- "PoC is deprecated" (false; both POV and POC coexist)
- "Elastic Cloud Agent Builder" (just "Agent Builder")
- "AI Assistant" alone (collides with shipped Elastic AI Assistant)
- "Hallucination-free" (say "grounded in your data")
- "Single pane of glass" (cliche)
- "Wrapper" (diminishes the work)
- "Sales rep replacement" (third rail)
- "MEDDPICC" by name (say "value-based selling")
- "Field Engineers are integration specialists" (excludes SA/CA audience)

## 11. The single sentence that summarizes the v4 rewrite

> v3 told the judge what FE Copilot is. v4 tells the judge what their Tuesday is going to feel like.

That is the difference between a feature tour and a keynote. Drill the cold open, trust the autopilot, land the CTA. The script is scored. Read the score, do not read the words.
