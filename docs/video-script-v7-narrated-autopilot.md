# FE Copilot demo video v7 (SKO May 2026 - narrated autopilot)

> 3:00 single take. v7 is a surgical change from v6: the 50-second autopilot beat now has narration over the top instead of silence. Everything else (cold open, Kulkarni quote, "View agent in Kibana" click, demo data dashboards, the back-to-back-meetings bookend, CTA) stays exactly the same as v6.
>
> **Pick v6 if** you trust 50 seconds of silence to land as confidence and want the demo to feel cinematic.
> **Pick v7 if** 50 seconds of silence feels long to YOU during rehearsal - because if it feels long to you, it'll read as awkward to the judge. Your delivery confidence is the load-bearing variable.

---

## What changed vs v6

Two beats. Everything else is verbatim from v6.

| Beat | v6 | v7 |
|---|---|---|
| **B2 Autopilot (0:30 - 1:20)** | 50 s silent | 50 s narrated. ~95 words across 50 s with three natural pauses where the screen does the talking. The narration NAMES each step as it renders, in plain SA-to-SA voice. |
| **B3 Pivot (1:20 - 1:35)** | "Fifty seconds. A brief, a discovery plan, a Splunk TCO, and a real agent built into Kibana - all of it, while you finished your coffee." Then the go-to-place positioning. | The artifact list moves into B2 (because we name them as they render). B3 now opens with **"That was fifty seconds."** and goes straight into the go-to-place positioning. Tighter. |

If you were running v6 and want to switch to v7, you only need to rehearse two beats. Everything else is muscle memory from v6.

## 1. The narrated autopilot (B2, 0:30 - 1:20)

50 seconds. ~95 words. Three deliberate pauses where the screen does the talking. Cadence: conversational, not voiceover. Speak like you're walking a colleague through your own workflow, not narrating a commercial.

```
[CAM A locked wide. Hands flat on the desk near the mouse. Click "Show me the magic."]

[0:30 - 0:36, autopilot fires, quick-research card begins to render]
Watch what happens.
One click - and FE Copilot starts pulling the customer's context together.

[0:36 - 0:46, the pre-meeting brief renders, AutoOps cluster signals card appears inside it]
First, the brief comes in -
their last earnings, their stack, the Splunk renewal on their desk,
and the AutoOps cluster signals showing how their environment is actually running right now -
all grounded in their data.

[0:46 - 0:58, Field Assistant renders 5 displacement questions]
Then the Field Assistant writes the discovery plan for you -
five questions, sequenced -
buyer, Splunk pain, DORA gap, champion, decision window.

[PAUSE half a beat - let the questions sit in frame.]

[0:58 - 1:13, Agent Builder modal opens, the Competitive bundle gets selected, "Splunk Displacement" appears in the agent list]
And while you're reading, it's building you the agent -
Splunk Displacement,
with the right tools selected, deployed straight into your Kibana cluster.

[PAUSE one full beat as the completion card lands.]

[1:13 - 1:20, completion card visible, lean back]
That agent did not exist a minute ago.
```

**Word count**: ~95 words across 50 seconds. That's 114 wpm in the narrated portions, which is keynote cadence with deliberate pauses, not podcast speed. **Do not rush.** The pauses are part of the script - they let each artifact land in frame before you name the next one.

**Why it works**: Each line is timed to a render moment, not to an arbitrary clock. You're naming what the audience is seeing, in the order they see it - exactly the way an SA would walk a colleague through their actual workflow. The two pauses (after the discovery questions, after the agent appears) are where the silence does the work; they're the only two moments the audience needs to absorb without your voice.

**The hardest line to deliver**: "That agent did not exist a minute ago." It needs the same weight as "Eighteen minutes" in the cold open. Drop the volume slightly. Do not smile. Let it land.

## 2. The new pivot (B3, 1:20 - 1:35)

Same total length as v6. The artifact recap moves out (because B2 already named them). What's left is pure positioning.

```
[CUT to CAM B at 1:20. Smile lands first. One full beat of eye contact.]

That was fifty seconds.

[PAUSE half a beat.]

The idea is simple: this is meant to be the go-to place,
so a Solutions Architect can prep a discovery call for a new prospect
and a Customer Architect can walk into a back-to-back meeting already in sync
with what the customer needs -
without flipping through twelve tabs and a Salesforce note from three weeks ago.

Let me show you the moving parts.
```

**Why it works**: "That was fifty seconds" is the smallest possible recap - three words that close the autopilot beat without listing the artifacts again (because we already named them as they rendered). The rest is the v6 positioning thesis verbatim, which is the line every SA and CA in the audience nods at.

## 5. Body language cues for B2 (the only beat with new cues)

- **0:30 "Watch what happens."** - small forward lean, palm flat on desk. You are inviting the judge into the demo, not announcing it.
- **0:36 "First, the brief comes in"** - nod toward the screen once, not at the camera. Your eyes follow the brief as it renders.
- **0:46 "Then the Field Assistant writes the discovery plan for you"** - small open-palm gesture toward the screen on "writes the discovery plan."
- **0:58 PAUSE after the questions** - half a beat of stillness. Let the audience read one or two of the questions before you keep going. This is the most important pause in B2.
- **1:00 "And while you're reading, it's building you the agent"** - slight smile. The line is meant to land as a gentle "wait, there's more" - not as a tour-guide reveal.
- **1:13 "That agent did not exist a minute ago."** - drop the volume by 15 percent. Do not smile. Hold eye contact with the camera, not the screen, for the full sentence.

## 6. v6 vs v7 - the trade-off, explicitly

| Dimension | v6 (silent) | v7 (narrated) |
|---|---|---|
| **Risk** | If your delivery confidence wavers during 50 s of silence, the take is dead. Silence amplifies any awkwardness. | If your pacing slips by 5+ seconds, you'll run over 3:00. The narration has no buffer. |
| **Reward** | The silence reads as confidence the way only the most senior keynotes do. Judges remember the demo where the speaker stopped talking. | Every artifact gets named in real time, which makes the demo feel **dense with content** rather than light on it. Judges remember the demo that did the most in three minutes. |
| **Best for** | A speaker who has rehearsed the silent take 8+ times and feels at home in it. | A speaker who finds 50 seconds of silence physically uncomfortable, OR who wants every second of the video doing rhetorical work. |
| **Cinematic feel** | High. | Medium. |
| **Information density** | Medium. | High. |

**My recommendation**: rehearse both, twice each, then pick whichever leaves you feeling more in command at the end of B2. The judge doesn't see your rehearsal - they see your delivery. Your delivery is what wins. Pick the version you can land.
